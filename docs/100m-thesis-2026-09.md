# Watchora — The $100M Thesis (2026-09)

Not a promise — the arithmetic that makes Watchora a venture-scale or
acquisition-scale asset, and the milestones that unlock it. Pair with
`docs/growth-plan-2026-09.md` (go-to-market) and
`docs/beat-the-competitors-2026-09.md` (moat).

## 1. Why this is a $100M-category company

- **Market**: ≥2.2B people with vision impairment (WHO); $411B/yr global
  productivity loss; 8–10% CAGR in assistive tech for visual impairment.
- **The unlock Watchora has that incumbents don't**: assistive software for
  this population has historically been (a) $500–$4,500 hardware, (b) free
  corporate side-projects that get sunset, or (c) human services priced per
  minute. A free PWA that runs on a $60 Android phone in Hindi, Tamil, or
  Spanish opens the **95% of the market that incumbents' pricing and
  platforms structurally exclude** — starting with India.
- **The payer insight**: the blind user's family pays. Caregiver tools turn
  a low-income end user into a $200–400/yr household subscription — and
  every user organically enrolls 1–3 caregivers (built-in K-factor).
- **The data moat**: community hazard reports compound city by city into
  the only ground-truth accessibility dataset that isn't owned by a map
  giant. That dataset is licensable (cities, transit, delivery/logistics
  routing) and defensible precisely because it is slow to build.

## 2. Revenue lines to $100M (per year, at scale)

| Line | Mechanism | Plausible scale |
|---|---|---|
| Family Plan subscriptions | caregiver safety, live location, escalation alerts | 500k households × $240/yr = **$120M ceiling**; 100k × $240 = $24M first real milestone |
| B2B accessibility suite | Be My Eyes "Service AI" model: companies pay so their blind customers get instant accessible support | $10–50k per contract; 200 contracts = $2–10M |
| Hazard-data licensing | cities/transit/logistics buy the accessibility layer NaviLens sells hardware for | $250k–1M per city deployment |
| Grants & public contracts | SBIR, Google.org, EU Accessibility Act procurement, India ADIP | non-dilutive runway |

The $100M line sits at the intersection: ~100k paying households plus
double-digit B2B contracts plus 2–3 city licenses — achievable at ~2–4M
active free users, which is <2% of India's blind population alone.

## 3. Why Google / Musk-scale acquirers would care

- **Google**: Android Accessibility is strategic (TalkBack, Look to Speak,
  Guided Frame). Watchora gives them (a) the emerging-market, Indic-language
  assistive layer they lack, (b) the hazard/accessibility dataset, (c) a
  team fluent in a user group they serve but rarely hear from. Sound size:
  Android ships on 3B+ devices; a camera-assist layer that works on $60
  phones is a default-app candidate.
- **xAI / Tesla / Apple-style acquirers**: real-world multimodal perception
  + safety-critical human factors + an on-device-first architecture is the
  same problem shape as embodied AI and autonomy — Watchora is a dataset and
  a talent pipeline for "AI that moves through the physical world safely."
- **What makes us acquirable rather than absorbable**: deterministic safety
  layer (testable, no prompt-magic liability), honest product claims, a
  community that trusts us, and the durability commitment — all of which we
  have been deliberately engineering since the first audit.

## 4. The milestones (what actually earns the valuation)

1. **10k real users** with D7 ≥ 25% (India pilot cohorts) — product proof.
2. **1k paying families** — willingness-to-pay proof.
3. **First B2B accessibility contract** — enterprise proof.
4. **Hazard dataset: 50k moderated reports in ≥5 cities** — moat proof.
5. **SOC2/VPAT + published accuracy benchmarks** — procurement proof.
6. **Native app with background journey monitoring** — platform proof.

Everything currently in the repo (audit remediations, notifications, honest
demo mode, install guide, daily-living channels, companion) maps to
milestones 1–2. The next engineering tranche (follow-up Q&A, barcode scan,
find-my-things) maps to retention inside milestone 1.

## 5. Honest risks to the thesis

- Apple/Google ship natively what we build (mitigation: we are the
  cross-platform + Indic + caregiver layer; also acquisitions DO happen for
  exactly this).
- Missed-hazard liability (mitigation: fail-silent design, deterministic
  layer, published benchmarks, no safety guarantees in copy).
- Free-tier AI inference cost (mitigation: on-device YOLO/OCR offload,
  rate limits, cache).
- PWA discovery ceiling (mitigation: native wrapper by milestone 3).
